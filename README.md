# Causes of Mortality in Humanitarian Crisis: A Scoping Review

## About the study

Humanitarian crises can result in substantial direct and indirect mortality. However, evidence on the causes of death across different humanitarian settings is often fragmented and reported using varied study designs, data sources and mortality measures.

This scoping review aims to map and synthesise evidence on the leading causes of mortality in humanitarian crises worldwide.

## Research questions

The review addresses the following questions:

1. What are the leading causes of death in humanitarian crises?
2. How do mortality patterns vary across different settings?
3. What methodological limitations and evidence gaps exist?

## Inclusion criteria

### Geography

Data must have been collected in one or more of the 36 countries included in the review that were or are experiencing a humanitarian crisis, as indicated by the existence of a Humanitarian Response Plan.

### Study period

Data must have been collected from 2015 onwards.

Data must also have been collected during or after the first year in which the relevant country had a Humanitarian Response Plan.

### Outcome

The study must report one or more of the following as an outcome:

- Death
- Mortality
- Fatality
- Survival
- Excess mortality
- Mortality burden
- Disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), where death is a component
- Years of life lost (YLLs)

### Study population

Human studies only.

## Humanitarian Response Plans

A Humanitarian Response Plan, or HRP, is a coordinated, UN OCHA-led plan produced for a country or crisis when the scale of humanitarian need requires an organised, multi-agency response.

The existence of an HRP indicates that a setting meets the review's threshold for a humanitarian crisis requiring a coordinated response.

### Why this matters for screening

Studies are only eligible if they report data from one or more of the eligible countries during or after the first year in which the country had an HRP.

This requirement connects the study and its reported mortality outcomes to a qualifying humanitarian crisis.

## Exclusion criteria

Exclude studies when:

- They were conducted in a country that did not have its first HRP before or during the study period.
- The reported data were collected before 2015.
- They do not report death, mortality, fatality, survival, excess mortality or mortality burden as an outcome.
- They do not involve human populations, such as animal-only or laboratory studies.
- Mortality outcomes cannot be attributed to a specific eligible country or a more granular geographical unit within that country.

## Important screening notes

### Systematic reviews

Include systematic reviews that report or synthesise mortality evidence for one or more eligible HRP countries.

Tag these studies separately in the screening sheet for subsequent identification and analysis.

### Modelling studies

Include modelling studies only when their mortality findings are based on empirical data.

Exclude studies based entirely on simulations, hypothetical scenarios or future projections without empirical mortality data.

### Global and multi-country studies

Global or multi-country studies are eligible only when mortality results are reported separately for one or more eligible HRP countries.

Exclude studies when results are presented only as a combined global, regional or multi-country estimate and cannot be attributed to an individual eligible country.
