# Causes of Mortality in Humanitarian Crisis: A Scoping Review

## Study overview

Humanitarian crises can result in substantial direct and indirect mortality. However, evidence on the causes of death across different humanitarian settings is often fragmented and reported using varied study designs, data sources and mortality measures.

This scoping review aims to map and synthesise evidence on the leading causes of mortality in humanitarian crises worldwide.

## Research questions

This review addresses the following questions:

1. What are the leading causes of death in humanitarian crises?
2. How do mortality patterns vary across different settings?
3. What methodological limitations and evidence gaps exist?

## Eligibility criteria

### Geography

Studies must report data collected in one or more of the 36 countries included in the review that were or are experiencing a humanitarian crisis, as indicated by the existence of a Humanitarian Response Plan.

### Study period

Data must have been collected from 2015 onwards.

Studies must also have been conducted during or after the first year in which the relevant country had a Humanitarian Response Plan.

### Outcome

Studies must report one or more of the following as an outcome:

- Death
- Mortality
- Fatality
- Survival
- Excess mortality
- Mortality burden
- Disability-adjusted life years, where death is a component
- Years of life lost

### Study population

Human studies only.

## Humanitarian Response Plans

A Humanitarian Response Plan, or HRP, is a coordinated plan led by the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs for a country or crisis where the scale of humanitarian need requires an organised, multi-agency response.

The existence of an HRP indicates that a setting meets the review's threshold for a humanitarian crisis requiring a coordinated response.

### Why this matters for screening

Studies are only eligible when they were conducted in one or more of the countries included in the review during or after the period in which that country first had an HRP.

This requirement is what connects the study and its reported mortality outcomes to a qualifying humanitarian crisis.

## Main exclusion criteria

Studies should be excluded when:

- They were conducted in a country that did not have its first HRP before or during the study period.
- The reported data were collected before 2015.
- They do not report death, mortality, fatality, survival, excess mortality or mortality burden as an outcome.
- They do not involve human populations, such as animal-only or laboratory studies.
- Mortality outcomes cannot be attributed to a specific eligible country or a more granular geographic unit within that country.

## Important screening guidance

### Systematic reviews

Include systematic reviews that report or synthesise mortality evidence for one or more eligible HRP countries.

These studies should be tagged separately in the screening sheet so that they can be identified during the subsequent stages of the review.

### Modelling studies

Include modelling studies only when their mortality findings are based on empirical data.

Exclude studies that are based entirely on simulations, hypothetical scenarios or future projections without empirical mortality data.

### Global and multi-country studies

Global or multi-country studies are eligible only when mortality results are reported separately for at least one eligible HRP country.

Exclude studies when the results are reported only as a combined regional, global or multi-country estimate and cannot be attributed to an individual eligible country.
